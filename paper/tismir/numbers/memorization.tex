\paragraph{No verbatim recall.} No model reproduces any canary from its 12-note prompt, and no model reproduces a real training melody, including members of families with ten or more copies in the raw corpus (one of 150 single-copy melodies is extracted in each small-pool run). With transposition augmentation, canary exposure stays within 0.1 nats per note of zero at every insertion level and every model size, rising only slightly for canaries inserted 32 times (+0.03 at 1.8M parameters, +0.07 to +0.09 at 7.4M). This holds even on the small pool, where a canary inserted 32 times is seen about 400 times.

\paragraph{Augmentation is what suppresses memorization.} Removing the augmentation changes the picture. On the standard pool, exposure of the canaries inserted 32 times rises from +0.03 to +0.20 nats per note at 1.8M parameters\input{numbers/mem_noaug_m.tex}. On the small pool it rises steadily with repetition, to +0.35 at eight insertions and +0.97 at 32. These models prefer the canaries they have seen, by an amount that grows with duplication and with model size, as language models do \citep{carlini2023quantifying,kandpal2022dedupprivacy}; they still cannot reproduce 20 notes of one exactly. Random transposition shows the model each canary in a different key every pass, which turns an absolute pitch string into an interval pattern that has to be learned rather than stored. On PDMX the augmentation also costs likelihood (1.66 against 1.78 nats per note at 1.8M, 1.44 against 1.51 at 7.4M), because key is informative in this corpus, so the choice between the two trades test likelihood against memorization.

\paragraph{Copying in free samples is figuration.} Free samples often share long runs with some training melody: 22\% of samples from the 1.8M raw model and 29\% from the 7.4M raw model contain a run of at least 20 notes, against 8\% for the 1.8M model trained on the deduplicated corpus. Every such run we inspected is a repeated note, a trill, an arpeggio loop or a scale exercise, figures that occur in thousands of scores. Among melodic passages, the longest that any sample shares with any training melody is 15 to 20 notes in every run, and the longest of those are themselves loops entered from a different note. Most 14- and 15-note overlaps are stock phrases shared by many folk dance tunes and songs.

\paragraph{Reading the two studies together.} At the scale typical of symbolic melody models, transformers trained with the usual augmentation do not store their training melodies in a retrievable form, yet they still score leaked test melodies better than clean ones by an amount that grows with capacity (Section~\ref{sec:counterfactual}). A leak can therefore inflate a benchmark without any detectable memorization, and a check for verbatim copies is no substitute for a check of the split.
